What to Expect at a Padel Camp: A Day-by-Day Breakdown

By The Padel Camp team · October 26, 2026

Most people who sign up for a padel camp have the same question: what will I actually be doing for five days? It is a fair question. "Camp" can mean anything from a week of drills with forty other players to a few friendly matches with a coach watching from the side.

This is how a structured camp works at The Padel Camp in Limassol, and what you can expect from each day.

Before day one: knowing where you start

Good training starts with an honest picture of your game. On the first session, coaches watch you play and look at the basics: grip, footwork, positioning at the net and at the back, how you use the walls, and which shots you avoid.

This is not a test you pass or fail. It is how coaches decide what to work on with you, and how groups are formed, so you train with players close to your level.

Day 1: assessment and groups

You warm up, play, and get your first feedback. By the end of the session you know two or three things that matter most in your game right now. For most players this list is shorter and more specific than they expect.

Days 2–3: technique

The middle of the week is where most of the change happens. Sessions focus on the shots and movements that came up in your assessment: the volley, the bandeja, defending off the back glass, the lob, the serve and return.

With a maximum of four players per court, you hit a lot of balls and get corrected while the movement is still fresh. That is the main difference from a group class, where feedback is shared between eight or more people.

Day 4: tactics and decisions

Technique only matters if you use the right shot at the right moment. On day four the focus moves to decisions: when to go to the net, when to lob, how to play with your partner, what to do under pressure. Situations from real matches are played out and repeated until the choice starts to feel natural.

Day 5: match play and final evaluation

The last day puts it all together in match play. Coaches watch again and compare with day one. You leave with individual feedback: what improved, what to keep practising, and what to focus on in your regular games.

What five days will and won't do

Five days won't turn you into a different player. They will make you a more conscious one. You will understand why you lose certain points, which habits cost you the most, and what to practise next. Most players say that this clarity is worth more than any single new shot.

Practical details

  • Where: MyQPadel, Limassol. Fully covered courts, café and pro shop on site.
  • Group size: maximum four players per court.
  • What to bring: your racket, padel shoes, water, a towel, and a change of clothes.
  • Level: camps work best for players who already play regularly and want to improve, from improving beginners to intermediate players.
