Morning, Evening or Weekend: Which Padel Training Format Fits Your Schedule

By The Padel Camp team · October 26, 2026

The biggest obstacle to structured training is rarely motivation. It is the calendar. Most adult players in Limassol work, travel or run a business, and a week of daytime sessions is simply not possible.

That is why The Padel Camp now runs in three formats. They follow the same training philosophy and differ only in when and how long you train. Many of our players asked for exactly this, so we built the schedule around them.

The three formats at a glance

All three take place at MyQPadel in Limassol, on fully covered courts, with a maximum of four players per court.

Morning Camp: the full week, with the afternoon free

Three hours every morning for five days. You train when your energy is highest and still have the afternoon for work, family or rest. This format gives the most time for technique and the clearest progress across the week.

Choose it if you control your own schedule, work remotely with some flexibility, or want to combine training with a few days off.

Evening Camp: the full programme after work

The same 15 hours, from 18:00 to 21:00. You keep your working day as usual and train after it. Covered courts mean the conditions are the same every evening, whatever the weather.

Choose it if you work office hours and want the complete five-day programme without taking leave.

Weekend Camp: focused and compact

Six hours over Saturday and Sunday, in two groups. The programme is shorter, so it concentrates on the key points from your assessment and gives you a clear plan for what to practise afterwards.

Choose it if weekdays are full, or if you want to try structured training before committing to a full week.

How to decide

  • Want the deepest progress? Choose a five-day format, morning or evening, whichever fits your work.
  • Can't take time off? Evening Camp gives you the full programme after work.
  • Weekdays impossible? Weekend Camp gives you a focused start.
  • Not sure about your level? All formats begin with an assessment, and groups are formed by level.
