Five Technical Mistakes Intermediate Padel Players Make

By The Padel Camp team · October 26, 2026

At the intermediate level, most lost points don't come from a lack of talent. They come from a handful of habits that were fine when you started and now hold you back. Here are the five our coaches see most often, and how to start working on each.

1. Hitting the ball too late

Many players wait for the ball instead of meeting it. The contact point ends up behind the body, the shot loses control, and volleys float. The fix starts with the feet: small adjustment steps before every shot, and contact in front of you, where you can see the ball and the racket together.

Try this: in your next game, focus only on where you make contact. Ask a partner to tell you when you hit a ball beside or behind your body.

2. Using too much backswing

Padel is a game of short, compact movements. A big tennis-style backswing costs time, especially at the net, and makes it harder to control direction. Most volleys need very little preparation: the racket stays in front, and the movement comes from the shoulder and the step, not the wrist.

3. Turning the bandeja into a smash

The bandeja is a control shot. Its job is to keep the net position, not to win the point. Hitting it too hard, too flat or too close to the glass usually gives the opponents an easy ball. A good bandeja is played sideways to the net, with a slice, aimed deep and low, often towards the corner.

4. Running away from the back glass

Instead of letting the ball come off the wall, many players try to hit it before it reaches the glass, often in an uncomfortable position. Learning to turn, step back with the ball and wait for it to come off the wall gives you more time, not less. It is the shot that changes defence most.

5. Leaving the net too easily

The team at the net usually controls the point. Yet intermediate players often give that position away: going back for a lob they could have played as a bandeja, or not coming forward after a good lob of their own. Ask yourself after every point: did we hold the net, and if we lost it, why?

Why these are hard to fix alone

All five feel natural from the inside. You don't notice a late contact point or an oversized backswing until someone shows you. That is why feedback in the moment, with many repetitions of the same shot, works faster than months of matches.
